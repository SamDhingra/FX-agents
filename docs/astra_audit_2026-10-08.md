**Audit date:** 8 October 2026. **Verdict:** no demonstrated deployable edge; repair execution accounting and establish research/live parity before further selection or promotion.

**Revision 1 — Jev integration (8 October 2026):** TypeSafe Jev is a required part of the proposed design. This revision replaces the blanket logging-only recommendation with bounded contextual selection, explicit forecasts, calibration and staged validation. These are design changes, not deployed changes; the original source findings and verification results remain unchanged.

## 1. Executive summary

- **Do not promote this system to real money.** Execution reconciliation can fabricate journal profit, repeat partial closes, and lose successful fills when a later operation fails. A passing strategy screen cannot compensate for incorrect position state.
- **There is no demonstrated edge to deploy.** The reported near-flat year and negative 90 days remain reasons to stop promotion. The defects do not justify assuming hidden profitability.
- Research and live do not represent one system: research ignores configured bias weights; the trend profile exits fully in research but partially live; the learner uses an older, spread-free simulator.
- Restart resets risk baselines and halt state. The 15:50 flatten depends on incoming bars, with no independent deadline watchdog.
- Legacy pivot reduction is noncausal. Current bias structure uses a better causal implementation; do not label every strategy or the entire bias engine repainting.
- The 90-day run used a **30-day** selection-window setting despite prose saying 60, and included watch-only FX in its portfolio. Its headline and monthly totals also use different R definitions.
- Thousands of dependent cells, small samples, repeated selection and weak lab gates are mining noise. Shrinkage is useful regularization, not a multiple-testing correction.
- **Use Jev as a required adaptive selector of strategy × instrument × timeframe**, first in an active shadow policy and then in constant-risk practice after validation. Replace raw-grade sizing with explicit outcome forecasts, empirical calibration and net-expectancy selection; the current quality grade is not a win probability.
- The original ASTRA specification was not implemented exactly: its broker scope, event calendar, cost gates and parts of its state machine differ.
- Spend the next two weeks on correctness, reproducibility, the Jev selection experiment and three frozen alternative hypotheses. Two weeks cannot establish a trading edge.

## 3. Method critique, in priority order

### 3.1 Establish one executable experiment

Unify signal availability, entry eligibility, exits, costs, sizing, portfolio occupancy and journal accounting. Start with deterministic differential fixtures, then replay a short recorded practice stream through both live and research. Require identical decisions up to explicitly modeled asynchronous fill differences. A statistically sophisticated test of the wrong simulator is still the wrong test.

Every run needs: code SHA, effective non-secret config allowlist/hash, policy/spec versions, data hash and time span, bar completeness/DST convention, symbol metadata, cost schedule, event-calendar version, selection cutoff and experiment ID. Do not dump environment variables or config.local wholesale.

Keep the known reports as historical evidence. Recompute a small set of predefined baselines only after parity fixes; do not rerun the entire grid hoping corrections reveal winners. Report which results changed because of rules, accounting or data.

### 3.2 Treat playbook selection as the strategy under test

The shrinkage arithmetic is regularization, not a probability that a cell makes money. CellCube pools overlapping strategies/variants by instrument × timeframe × exit (94–128), so duplicated behaviors influence the empirical prior repeatedly. At n=30 and k_e=30, the mean is halfway toward the prior; searching thousands of these shrunken means still selects lucky winners.

The 0.50 index win-probability gate has no general economic justification. Net expectancy depends on average gain, average loss, costs and tail risk. With net mean win a and mean loss magnitude b, break-even win probability is b/(a+b); it need not be 50%. Do not punish a genuinely profitable low-win-rate trend strategy just to satisfy a win-rate preference.

The window is **observed trade days**, not calendar days: cube.days contains days appearing in trade records; i-window_days indexes that array. A configured 60 is generally about 12 trading weeks, potentially longer across no-trade days. The default walk-forward can start after 20 indexed days unless supplied another start. Name the units and require full warmup.

Use an initially fixed 252-session estimation window and monthly review for a small preregistered family set, with 60-session data reserved as a predefined regime/decay diagnostic. This is a starting design, not a newly optimized magic window. If an entry only works during one transient 60-day patch, the selector needs independent evidence that it can identify that patch before returns occur.

Evaluate the complete selection policy against (a) no trade, (b) a frozen simple baseline and (c) frozen equal-risk family allocation. Bootstrap and test **the policy**, including its selection and capacity logic. Cluster duplicate variants; never count correlated index positions or six exits of the same entry as independent evidence.

### 3.3 Correct for the research process

Freeze a trial ledger: all setups, exits, filters, discarded variants, policy thresholds and previous LLM-generated proposals. An “effective trial count” is not just today's retained cells.

- Use White's Reality Check, or a preregistered superior-predictive-ability alternative, on aligned daily strategy-minus-benchmark returns. Preserve simultaneous cross-instrument outcomes and dependence with session/week blocks; rerun the selector within resampled training histories. Report dependence/block-length sensitivity.
- Report deflated Sharpe on daily net account returns, including flat days, and disclose trial-count/correlation assumptions and non-normality. Do not feed a per-trade R t-stat into a Sharpe formula or use DSR to excuse leakage.
- For an exploratory candidate shortlist, use family-level multiplicity control and valid block-based p-values. Benjamini–Yekutieli is a conservative option under arbitrary dependence; ordinary BH needs justified dependence assumptions. Neither repairs p-values computed after unrecorded tuning.
- The current IID trade bootstrap (playbook.py:252–260) breaks clustered trading outcomes. Its fraction below zero is not “the chance the true edge is <=0,” despite year_test.py:79. Replace that label and show a dependence-aware confidence interval.
- CPCV is secondary. Use it to assess sensitivity across regimes after purging overlapping entry-to-exit label intervals and embargoing dependent boundaries. It can train on dates later than a test fold; therefore it is not a simulation of historical deployment. Keep chronological outer walk-forward plus a final untouched block as the primary evidence. Never count overlapping CPCV paths as independent observations.

The reports' broad skepticism about thousands of cells is justified. However, “300 survivors is exactly what chance predicts” needs an empirical null respecting dependent filters and variants. Similarly, “t above about 3 is probably real” in the year report is not a universal multiple-testing threshold.

Primary methodological sources: [White, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00152); [Bailey & López de Prado, 2014](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094); [Benjamini & Yekutieli, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1013699998). These methods quantify uncertainty; they create no trading edge.

### 3.4 Replace lab PASS with staged evidence

Thirty trades, both halves positive and t>=1.5 is an exploratory screen. A t of 1.5 is roughly a 7% one-sided tail event even before thousands of trials and dependence. Splitting the same selected history into two positive halves is not independent confirmation. lab.py:98 uses the pooled timestamp midpoint, so individual cells need not have balanced halves.

Rename current PASS to SCREENED. Promotion should require:

1. Causal, parity and accounting tests pass; correct data/calendar/cost provenance.
2. Frozen parameters and a genuinely untouched, chronological evaluation period; six months and 200 trades per intended instrument are minimum review screens, not sufficient evidence.
3. Positive after-cost expectancy with a selection-adjusted, session-block 95% lower confidence bound above zero and credible improvement over its simple control.
4. Positive expectancy under 2× execution-cost stress; acceptable mark-to-market drawdown; no single lucky day/instrument explaining the result.
5. Prospective practice execution long enough to reconcile fills, time exits and data loss. It validates integration, not real-money profitability.

For scale: detecting a 0.10R mean with 1R standard deviation at conventional two-sided 5% significance and 80% power needs roughly 800 independent observations. Dependence and selection make the requirement larger. Do not manufacture sample size by loosening setups.

The waiting candidates—smc_mss XAUUSD 3m; dtfx_wick_fib level=50–70 NDQ 15m, with/without validated-pullback swings; smc_ifvg XAUUSD 15m—remain research candidates. A 365-day run containing the 90 days used to choose them is **not** a 365-day OOS test. Separate the development interval, earlier unused history and future untouched interval. Earlier unused data is useful historical validation, but not forward deployment evidence. Do not retune after viewing the last block.

### 3.5 Jev as the required adaptive decision layer — revised recommendation

**Decision:** retain TypeSafe Jev and develop it into the strategy × instrument × timeframe selector. The earlier blanket “logging only” recommendation was too broad for this objective: the 70-trade report tests an existing signal-quality grade, not whether a properly designed Jev-assisted selector can identify conditional opportunities. It neither proves nor disproves that broader use. Keep the evidence objections, but change the architecture and experiment.

Jev must make an observable contribution: classify supplied context, assess candidate suitability and provide narrowly defined outcome forecasts. The selected policy then uses those outputs through a calibrated model and deterministic portfolio rules. While forecasts are unvalidated, run this as an **active shadow selector that chooses its own simulated trades**, alongside the baseline; this is more than collecting annotations. It must not increase real-money authority on the strength of the existing grade buckets.

**Terminology correction:** TypeSafe describes Jev as a System One model returning typed decisions, not a conversational text-generating LLM. Choice selects among named alternatives; Score evaluates a rubric; Noul supplies the probability of a specified yes/no proposition. TypeSafe recommends focused questions composed in code, and permits independent questions in one call. That fits this design better than asking one broad “which strategy will make money?” question. [Official introduction](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction), [primitives](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives), [API](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api).

#### B. Highest-value uses, in order

1. **Contextual strategy/timeframe selection.** Among a small vetted shortlist for XAUUSD, NDQ and US30, identify which approaches fit the supplied session context. Inputs should include measured volatility, directional efficiency, opening-range expansion, gap size, trend strength, spread/R, event proximity and historically out-of-sample conditional performance. 2. **Conditional forecast for an actual eligible signal.** Estimate a defined event, then calibrate that forecast on subsequent labels. Ask separate questions for profitability and an explicitly defined adverse excursion only if each has its own evaluation plan. Do not add a dozen untested scores.
3. **No-trade and context ambiguity.** Allow every candidate to be unsuitable. #### C. Define “probability” before designing the prompt

There are three different quantities:

| Quantity | Meaning | Correct use |
|---|---|---|
| Rubric score | Strength of “regime fit” or “setup quality” under named criteria. | Input feature/ranking diagnostic; **0.7 does not mean a 70% win rate**. |
| Outcome forecast | Probability that an explicit future event occurs under a frozen execution contract. | Noul output stored as raw forecast, subsequently checked/calibrated against realized labels. |
| Model confidence | Concentration of Choice/Score answer distribution; for Noul the app currently derives distance from .5. | Possible abstention/ambiguity feature after testing; not an independent estimate of profitable expectancy. |

TypeSafe documents confidence as a function of its answer distribution; Noul has no separate returned confidence. The existing abs(2p-1) conversion in jev.py:71 is consistent with the documentation's optional derived statistic, so that formula is **not itself a bug**. The error would be interpreting it as independent trading evidence. Vendor calibration claims do not establish calibration on this account's future trading outcomes. [Official confidence definition](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence).

**Primary candidate label, version JEV-NETPOS-v1:**

- Unit: one actually triggered, deterministic-policy-eligible candidate, identified by setup × variant × instrument × timeframe × exit-profile × side × signal timestamp.
- At the forecast snapshot, freeze the entry protocol, stop, management version, maximum hold, cost model and forced-exit/event deadlines.
- Define Y=1 only if a filled trade's total realized P&L under that contract is strictly positive after spread, slippage, commissions and applicable financing/adjustments; Y=0 for zero or negative net P&L. Horizon is until the specified stop/exit/time deadline, never “sometime later.”
- Forecast is explicitly **conditional on the specified order filling within its validity window**. Unfilled/rejected/expired orders are a separate label, not losing trades and not profitable forecasts. At policy level their reward is zero; fill probability and adverse selection must be measured separately.
- Do not conflate this with “hits 1R first,” “wins the next hour,” “has positive long-run expectancy” or “best strategy in the list.”
- Use realistic quote/execution labels where available. Counterfactual labels for unselected candidates are simulated and must be tagged as such, never reported as actual broker fills.

Example Noul instructions, with the referenced fields present in state:

> For state.candidates.c0, conditional on entry filling under its frozen execution protocol before order_expiry, will the complete trade finish with strictly positive net P&L under the specified stop, exit_profile, costs and force_exit_at? Use only information available at state.asof. This is a forecast of that one outcome, not a textbook-quality rating. Missing evidence should increase uncertainty.

Each question must name the full proposition and referenced candidate in its instructions. Question IDs alone are not model context. Independent batched questions do not see each other's answers; if a second judgment really needs an earlier result, compose in code or use a second call. [TypeSafe question construction](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives).

For **pre-session/hourly activation**, there is not yet an actual signal. Ask Jev about present context/suitability, not the profitability of an imaginary trade. If you want a block-level forecast later, register a different label: net USD policy return from all admitted entries in that activation block, with exits followed to completion and zero reward for blocks with no entries. This measures opportunity frequency as well as conditional trade quality. Do not train it using only blocks that happened to trade.

#### D. Proposed selector contract: ASTRA-JEV-SELECTOR-CFD-001 v0.1.0

**Universe:** the three permitted trading instruments; EURUSD/GBPUSD/AUDUSD remain research-only. A candidate always includes its exit profile. Initially retain at most six approved cells per instrument, at most two per family, chosen by a frozen training-only procedure. This cap is an engineering/research default, not an optimized result. Experimental cells can appear only in the shadow experiment until the lab's corrected evidence requirements are met.

**Decision flow:**

1. Deterministic code removes disallowed symbols, incomplete data, stale quotes, calendar blackouts, invalid stops, insufficient margin, risk halts and already-consumed opportunities. Do not spend Jev calls on these.
2. Build an immutable as-of feature snapshot and compact candidate descriptions. Include formula-computed regime features, exit rules, intended side, cost/R, remaining holding time, shrunk historical net expectancy, sample counts, session counts, uncertainty and last eligible training timestamp. No future trade outcomes, retrospective “trend day” labels or full-sample ranks.
3. Jev returns separate regime-fit Score and raw net-positive Noul forecasts for eligible triggered candidates. Optional context Choice includes trend/range/mixed-uncertain with frozen definitions. Choices of “best” are relative preferences, not absolute win probabilities; the first version does not trade directly from such a Choice.
4. A small regularized numerical selection model consumes Jev outputs plus the deterministic features. Compare it with an otherwise identical model that omits Jev. Its output is an empirically calibrated p_net_positive plus estimated conditional payoffs/uncertainty, with unsupported cases marked insufficient_evidence.
5. Estimate net positive-outcome mean g and nonpositive-outcome loss magnitude l from past-only, partially pooled data, conditioned on the same forecast/context design. Then expected net R = p*g - (1-p)*l. Because g and l are already net of costs, do not subtract those costs twice. Probability alone must not rank unequal exit profiles.
6. Choose the positive-utility eligible candidate with the strongest validated net expected value under the account's position/correlation constraints. No trade has utility zero. In a validated policy require the preregistered conservative utility criterion to clear zero; uncertainty is estimated by refitting the complete model/selector over session blocks, not by calling model confidence a confidence interval. In the initial shadow pilot use a frozen point-estimate rule but label it experimental.
7. Across different instruments compare expected net dollars under equal permitted risk reservations, and evaluate holding time/opportunity costs in the chronological account replay. Do not guarantee a winner for every instrument or timeframe. Existing open positions and the one-index rule can reject the highest standalone forecast.
8. Freeze an accepted trade's exit profile. Later Jev forecasts cannot mutate its stops or targets. Recheck current executable quote, deadlines and deterministic risk before actual submission.
9. Log every candidate, response, abstention, capacity rejection and final action, including the deterministic baseline's counterfactual choice.

**Probability is not profitability — arithmetic illustration only:** candidate A with p=.65, g=.50R and l=1R has expected -0.025R; B with p=.45, g=2R and l=1R has +0.35R. A win-probability-only router picks the wrong candidate. These are invented explanatory numbers, not forecasts for your bot.

**Calibration design:** use chronological training, later calibration and later untouched evaluation blocks; purge entry-to-exit overlaps. Start with a low-complexity regularized logistic model and a simple sigmoid calibrator where supported by data, with partial pooling across family/instrument rather than separate tiny models for every cell. Fit preprocessing, pooling and hyperparameters inside training only; fit calibration on disjoint past predictions. Isotonic calibration is a later alternative only with sufficient data. Do not use shuffled default cross-validation on overlapping trades. Proper scoring and reliability checks should include Brier score, log loss and reliability curves with bin counts/uncertainty; Brier improvement alone does not isolate calibration from discrimination. [Calibration reference](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html).

Seventy old quality grades are not training data for this new label/prompt. Begin collecting new forecasts. Fit no calibrator on the same rows used to claim its success. If predictions do not add ranking information beyond simple numeric features, calibration cannot manufacture that information. During cold start, any active shadow ranking must use a separately frozen experimental rule and clearly label its scores uncalibrated; it cannot publish invented probabilities or substitute its results for evaluation of the subsequently fitted P1/P2 models.

#### E. Efficient call placement and failure behavior

**Use a slow context layer and a bounded signal layer.**

- Refresh context five minutes before each active NY trading hour; optionally refresh at 09:25 for the cash open. Use only data available then. For a 09:00–15:30 pilot, 08:55 plus each :55 through 14:55 and 09:25 is eight refresh times: up to 24 requests/day at one per instrument, or fewer if batched. These are proposed scheduling counts, not measured API load.
- The context layer prepares regime fit and the approved shortlist for the next block. It cannot observe the next hour's bars. Do not exclude candidates from the evaluation ledger just because Jev dislikes them.
- At real signal times, micro-batch candidates sharing an as-of timestamp and complete instrument watermark. Set a bounded snapshot deadline; missing or late inputs make affected candidates unavailable, rather than waiting indefinitely or looking ahead. Ask independent candidate questions in one request where practical; preserve each candidate's own field path. Only this layer makes the concrete trade-outcome forecast.
- Start with a proposed cap of 120 signal batches/day for the three instruments. Count dropped/deferred batches and evaluate their effect. A budget cap is part of the policy, not missing data to omit from results.
- Send numerical features and short strategy definitions, not thousands of bars. Each candidate's sample counts and unsupported contexts must be visible. Do not ask Jev to calculate ATR, probabilities from frequencies, position size or significance.
- Cache on model ID + prompt/schema hash + candidate/exit version + as-of feature revision + portfolio/news/cost revision. Exact duplicate requests can reuse a response; a materially changed snapshot cannot reuse a prior forecast as if current.
- Context result expiry is the next scheduled refresh or an explicit material event: new information, blackout, data correction, volatility/cost bucket change or portfolio-state change. Signal forecasts have a two-second application-level validity budget from request submission for this initial design; if not usable in time, skip that entry. This is a service target to measure, not a claim about broker/model latency.
- Run Jev in a separate worker/queue. Target p95 response below one second and hard application deadline two seconds; record queue time, inference time, time to executable quote, timeout rate and cost per candidate. SDK retries must not extend entry validity. Risk supervision and protective exits never await Jev.
- Pin a versioned model ID and preserve the actual response model ID. Alias updates require a new evaluation/calibration version. TypeSafe explicitly notes that aliases can change behind unchanged client code. [Model-version documentation](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models).
- On timeout, invalid response, unavailable model or exhausted call budget: new Jev-dependent exposure is NO_TRADE in the initial pilot. Existing positions continue deterministic management. A cached valid context may remain informative, but cannot substitute for a missing required signal forecast.
- If a deterministic fallback policy is later allowed, give it a separate immutable ID, source label and performance series. Never insert heuristic outputs into the Jev calibration population or silently call them Jev probabilities.

The provider documents evaluating independent questions together; verify your own latency and token bill rather than assuming batching is free. The main efficiency gain is fewer redundant questions and better decisions per call, not maximizing call volume. [Batching guidance](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction).

### Shared execution/data contract

- Instruments are OANDA CFDs: NDQ = NAS100_USD, US30 = US30_USD, XAUUSD = XAU_USD, subject to account instrument qualification. Never reuse futures point values. EURUSD/GBPUSD/AUDUSD remain watch-only.
- All clocks use America/New_York local time with explicit DST and a versioned US cash-session calendar. Skip US cash holidays and early-close sessions for these first experiments. Missing required data/calendar/specification means no new exposure.
- One-minute bars are [t,t+1m); five-minute and hourly bars align to local clock boundaries. Use finalized bars only. Decision time is bar end; historical market entry is the first subsequent executable quote, or next 1m open with stated slippage when only OHLC exists.
- ATR14: TR=max(H-L, abs(H-Cprev), abs(L-Cprev)); first valid TR is H-L; seed with mean first 14 valid TRs, then Wilder (13*ATRprev+TR)/14. EMA(n): SMA first n closes, then alpha=2/(n+1). Require 500 completed bars of the longest indicator timeframe. Indicators persist across sessions; never invent bars during closures.
- q is qualified broker tick size. Long stop prices round down to q; short stops round up. Targets round toward entry. New stops become active only after the decision and successful broker acknowledgment.
- Baseline historical full spreads, solely to match existing reports: XAUUSD 0.40, NDQ 1.50, US30 2.50 price points. Add adverse slippage on each market/stop fill of max(q, 0.25*spread); resting targets have no favorable price improvement. These are **assumptions**, not measured OANDA execution. Actual bid/ask is preferred. Zero commission only if the qualified account schedule confirms it.
- With an unchanged full spread s, baseline round-trip price cost is approximately s+2*max(q,.25s), before commissions. Require estimated all-in cost / planned entry-to-stop price distance <=0.10. Stress spread and slippage together at 1.5× and 2×. Keep the original eligible trade set for primary stress reporting rather than silently filtering away stressed losers.
- Long resting stop triggers on bid, short on ask; adverse gaps execute worse. For OHLC ambiguity, protective stop precedes target, and record the ambiguity. No fills before decisions. No end-of-bar exact-close assumption when the decision uses that close.
- At proposal, use fresh executable quote, maximum age two seconds. Bound adverse entry movement at max(q,.25s) from that quote using broker-supported price protection. Cancel/reject if geometry or cost tests fail at submission. Log absent/stale quotes rather than substituting a historical mid. Historical one-minute approximation cannot certify two-second latency.
- Fixed research risk budget 0.10% of day-start USD NAV per accepted trade, including estimated transaction cost; at $70k this is $70. Round units down; include multiplier, currency conversion and margin. Use at most one combined NDQ/US30 position and one gold position, aggregate planned risk <=0.20%; maximum one filled trade per strategy/instrument/day; no pyramids or partials. Halt new entries after two net losing closes or a 0.5% day-start NAV loss, whichever comes first. These are experimental limits, not replacements for stricter existing controls.
- If simultaneous index proposals: lower estimated cost/R wins, exact tie NDQ. If strategy IDs also tie for the same symbol/time, lexicographic ID order; rejected proposals are not queued.
- Event schedule: no entries and liquidate at [event-15min,event+15min) for CPI, PPI, Employment Situation, GDP and PCE releases. Skip FOMC policy-decision days. Apply actual dated times and resume only on a new decision after blackout.

### ASTRA-ORB-EXP-CFD-001 — cash opening-range continuation

**Instruments/timeframe/session:** NDQ and US30, evaluated independently; 5m decisions, M1/quotes execution. Opening range [09:30,09:45); entry decisions 09:50 through 11:00 inclusive.

**Hypothesis:** a substantial cash-open range followed by a directional close-break may reflect continued price discovery. Broader risk distances and at most one entry reduce cost sensitivity. This is not the previously tested 08:00 morning-range/FVG strategy or multi-timeframe Bollinger entry.

**Exact rules:**
1. Freeze H_OR, L_OR and W=H_OR-L_OR at 09:45. M is median W over the preceding 20 complete regular cash sessions, excluding today. Require M>0 and 0.80<=W/M<=1.80.
2. Beginning at 09:50, the first 5m close outside [L_OR-q,H_OR+q] defines the only opportunity. Equality at boundary counts as a break: long C>=H_OR+q, short C<=L_OR-q.
3. Long additionally requires C>O and (C-L)/(H-L)>=0.75; short C<O and (H-C)/(H-L)>=0.75. Zero bar range fails. If the first break fails, DONE for the day.
4. Proposed initial stop is the opening-range midpoint: long floor(((H_OR+L_OR)/2)/q)*q; short ceil to q. With submitted executable entry E and current completed 5m ATR A, require 0.5A<=abs(E-S)<=2.0A and the shared cost gate. Submit next-event market entry; no reentry.
5. Freeze R=abs(actual fill-S). No profit target. At each subsequent 5m close, once the most favorable completed post-entry 5m close reaches E+1.5R for long (E-1.5R short), arm a trailing stop. Long: max(previous stop, highest post-entry 5m close - 2*current ATR5). Short: min(previous stop, lowest close + 2*ATR5). Round protectively; if proposed stop is already beyond executable market, close at market.
6. Exit on broker stop, event liquidation or 15:50, whichever occurs first.

**Costs/failure mode:** shared CFD assumptions. False opening breaks, late entries after an exhausted move and violent reversals can dominate. The range filter can exclude both quiet trends and useful large event days.

**Prove/kill test:** compare on untouched sessions against the same breakout without W/M and close-location filters, and against no trade; count all controls as trials. Require positive adjusted net evidence, positive 2×-cost expectancy and stability by year and index. Kill deployment if mean net R<=0 at the frozen evaluation endpoint or cost stress erases the edge; a CI spanning zero is unproven. Do not select the “best opening-range length” afterward.

### ASTRA-GAP-FAIL-CFD-001 — small overnight gap failure

**Instruments/timeframe/session:** NDQ and US30 separately; one decision at the 09:45 5m close; all exits by 11:00.

**Hypothesis:** limited overnight repricing that fails during the first cash-open interval may revert toward yesterday's cash close. This is a conditional failed-gap trade, not a claim that all gaps fill.

**Exact rules:**
1. P is previous regular cash session's 15:59–16:00 M1 close; O is today's 09:30 M1 open. G=O-P. D is ATR14 of completed cash-session daily bars [09:30,16:00), known before today. Require 0.10D<=abs(G)<=0.35D. Missing prior regular-session close or daily history means skip.
2. Compute opening H,L from [09:30,09:45) and C at 09:45.
3. Gap up: require P<C<=O-0.25G and H<=O+0.25G; propose short. Stop S=ceil((H+0.05D)/q)*q. Target T=P rounded up to q toward entry.
4. Gap down, g=abs(G): require O+0.25g<=C<P and L>=O-0.25g; propose long. Stop S=floor((L-0.05D)/q)*q. Target T=P rounded down to q toward entry.
5. At executable E require target ahead in trade direction, stop behind, planned reward/stop distance >=1.25 and shared cost gate. If target is already reached before entry, cancel. Enter at the first next quote with entry bound.
6. Full exit at target or stop; no trailing, partials or reentry. Otherwise exit first executable quote at 11:00 or earlier event liquidation.

**Costs/failure mode:** shared CFD assumptions. Genuine information gaps often continue; temporary opening reversal can be a trap. Broker index pricing/dividend adjustments can contaminate the apparent gap; exclude sessions with unmodeled adjustments and disclose exclusions from point-in-time records.

**Prove/kill test:** compare with an unconditional size-matched small-gap fade at 09:45, on the same eligible sessions and costs. Preregister upward/downward gap reporting. Fail promotion if the reversal condition provides no incremental net benefit, losses concentrate on a recurring unmodeled event, or 2× costs remove net expectancy. Do not optimize the gap bands on the final sample.

### ASTRA-H1-VOL-TREND-CFD-001 — slower volatility-scaled continuation

**Instruments/timeframe/session:** XAUUSD primary; NDQ is a separate counted experiment. H1 bars, decisions at 09:00,10:00,11:00,12:00,13:00 NY; M1/quote execution; flat 15:50.

**Hypothesis:** persistent movement can offer more gross distance per spread than 1m pattern entries. Volatility scaling controls exposure; it does not create alpha. Published long-horizon time-series momentum is a reason to research persistence, not proof that this intraday truncation works.

**Exact rules:**
1. On completed hourly bar t, A=ATR14[t]; calculate EMA32/EMA128 and ER24=abs(C[t]-C[t-24])/sum(j=t-23..t,abs(C[j]-C[j-1])). If denominator=0, ER=0.
2. Require ER24>=0.30 and 0.75<=A/median(ATR14[t-120:t])<=1.75; median excludes current hour and contains 120 completed hourly observations.
3. Long: EMA32[t]>EMA128[t], EMA32[t]>EMA32[t-3], and C[t]>=max(H[t-12:t])+q, with previous 12 bars excluding t. Short mirrors all comparisons and uses prior 12 lows.
4. First qualifying hour is the sole daily opportunity. At current executable reference E0, initial stop is E0-2A long or E0+2A short, rounded away from entry. Require cost gate, submit bounded market order with stop on fill, then record actual initial R.
5. No profit target. After a subsequent completed H1 close first reaches +1R, arm a trailing stop. Long: max(previous stop, highest post-entry H1 close-2*current A). Short mirrors. Stop ratchets only on completed hourly decisions; if newly computed stop is through market, close immediately at executable price.
6. Exit stop, event liquidation or 15:50; no reversal/reentry that day.

**Costs/failure mode:** shared CFD assumptions. Whipsaw, stale multiday trends and insufficient time before flatten can erase the gross edge. Low frequency may require years of data. A 2ATR stop can be large, leaving low unit counts or no affordable trade.

**Prove/kill test:** compare with the same H1 breakout without EMA/ER/volatility filters and with a fixed-time trend-only control, using common costs/risk. Count both controls. Evaluate XAU and NDQ independently. Fail promotion if filters add no reliable value, returns depend on one regime, or 15:50 truncation leaves nonpositive stressed net expectancy.

### Is intraday the right horizon?

The account size does not establish an intraday advantage. Your time constraint rules out much of the horizon where broad trend evidence is strongest. Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen's time-series momentum evidence concerns diversified futures/forwards and long return horizons, not a one-hour CFD rule: [primary research summary](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Time-Series-Momentum).

If these small, frozen experiments fail, the economically sensible choices are no trading or a separately authorized longer-horizon mandate with overnight financing, gap risk and diversification explicitly modeled. Do not silently let an intraday bot become a swing bot. I would not spend more effort forcing a daily trade quota out of three instruments.
